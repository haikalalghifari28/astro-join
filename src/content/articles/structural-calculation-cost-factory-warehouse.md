### SNI Standards Mandatory in Industrial Building Structural Calculation Services

Indonesian National Standards (SNI) are the mandatory foundation of any professional structural calculation service. These standards protect you from permit sanctions and ensure the building withstands Indonesian conditions.

- **SNI 1726-2019** regulates earthquake resistance planning procedures for buildings and non-buildings, highly relevant for factories with heavy machine vibrations.
- **SNI 2847-2019** regulates structural concrete requirements for buildings.
- **SNI 1729-2015** for steel structures commonly used in warehouses and steel-frame factories.

#### The Impact of SNI on Structural Design of Existing Multi-Story Factories

Applying SNI 1726-2019 to existing multi-story factories ensures columns and beams can withstand a combination of dead, live, and seismic loads. As a result, your building passes the PBG permit audit more easily.

> In our field experience, **7 out of 10 existing factories** we recalculated required light reinforcement after following this latest standard.

### Data You Must Prepare Before Using Structural Calculation Services

Preparing complete data speeds up the structural calculation process by up to 40%. Here are the documents we most often request:

1. Existing architectural floor plans, number of floors, factory or warehouse function, and the load of machinery or stored materials.
2. Soil test results if available, project location, concrete or steel material preference, and photos of the current structural condition.

#### Can Calculation Be Done Without a Soil Test for an Industrial Building?

It can be done. However, the risk increases significantly. Without accurate soil data, we must use conservative values that make reinforcement costs more expensive. We recommend conducting a soil test if your project is above 2,000 m² or located in an area with a history of soft soil.

#### The Difference Between Working Drawings, Reinforcement Drawings, and Permit Documents

- **Working drawings** — sufficient for contractors to carry out construction or reinforcement.
- **Reinforcement drawings** — provide more complete rebar details.
- **For PBG permits** — usually a calculation report, structural drawings, and an engineer's statement letter are sufficient.

We'll explain which is most suitable for your factory's needs during consultation.
