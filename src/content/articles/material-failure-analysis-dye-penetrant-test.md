Material Failure Analysis is the process of detecting hidden defects in machine components before they cause major damage. **Dye Penetrant Test** allows for quick and accurate identification of micro-cracks.

We understand how concerning sudden cracks in your factory machinery can be. Through Dye Penetrant Test, we help identify micro-defects before they become catastrophic failures.

## The Material Failure Investigation Process in the Field

We always begin material failure investigation with a thorough visual inspection at the factory site. We then proceed with the appropriate non-destructive testing. Every stage is carried out with strict procedures to ensure accurate results. We have applied this approach dozens of times across various industrial factories.

### Preparation and Initial Crack Identification

1. **Component cleaning** — We thoroughly clean components of oil, dust, and rust. This cleaning is crucial so the penetrant can enter the crack.
2. **Visual inspection** — Next, we perform a visual inspection with the aid of light and a magnifying glass.
3. **Marking** — Suspicious surface defects are immediately marked.

### Proper Application of Dye Penetrant Test

1. **Penetrant application** — We evenly apply red penetrant to the component surface.
2. **Dwell time** — The dwell time we use is typically **10 to 20 minutes** depending on the material type.
3. **Cleaning** — Excess penetrant is then carefully cleaned off.
4. **Developer application** — We then spray white developer. Defects will appear as clear red indications within a short time.

> **In short, the accuracy of Dye Penetrant Test heavily depends on proper preparation and penetration time.**

## Integrating Magnetic Particle Inspection for More Accurate Results

We integrate **Magnetic Particle Inspection (MPI)** when components are made of ferromagnetic material. This method is highly sensitive at detecting subsurface cracks not visible via Dye Penetrant Test.

| Method                           | Advantage                                   | Limitation                            |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Dye Penetrant Test**           | Detects surface cracks, easy to apply       | Only for cracks open to the surface   |
| **Magnetic Particle Inspection** | Detects subsurface cracks, highly sensitive | Only for ferromagnetic materials      |
| **Combination of Both**          | Increases detection up to **95%**           | Requires more equipment and expertise |

We always use this approach on critical components such as **shafts** and **gearboxes**.

## Case Study: Cracking in Machine Components at a Factory

A cement factory in Central Java faced repeated oil leaks in its main gearbox. The maintenance team had replaced the seal several times, but the problem kept recurring. We were called in to conduct a material failure analysis on site.

### Discovery of an Overlooked Micro-Defect

After a thorough inspection, we found a network of fine cracks in the gearbox housing. **Dye Penetrant Test successfully revealed a defect previously invisible**:

- A micro-crack of **0.8 mm** in length previously missed by routine inspection
- The crack originated from **fatigue cracking** due to excessive vibration
- We recommended immediate repair and a design modification for vibration dampening

> **Result:** After the repair, the gearbox has operated normally to this day without recurring issues.
