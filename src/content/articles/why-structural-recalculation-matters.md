Every building is designed based on certain assumptions about material quality, working loads, and environmental conditions at the time of construction. However, over time, the actual condition of a building can change...

## 🔍 Why Is Recalculation Necessary?

### 1. Change of Building Function

When a building is repurposed — for example, from an office into a data center or heavy storage space — the working loads can change drastically.

- Live load increases (e.g., storage racks)
- Dead load increases (e.g., new partitions)
- Layout changes affect load distribution

### 2. Material Aging

Materials such as concrete and steel degrade over time. Contributing factors include:

- **Steel reinforcement corrosion** due to exposure to water and air
- **Concrete cracking** from thermal-load cycles
- **Reduced material strength** due to chemical reactions

### 3. Changes in Environmental Loads

Climate change and new regulations (such as for earthquakes) can render old design standards inadequate.

> **Expert Note:** According to SNI 1726:2019, Indonesia's seismic hazard map has been updated, meaning older buildings may no longer meet current earthquake safety standards.

## 📊 Recalculation Methods

The structural recalculation process includes:

1. **Non-Destructive Testing** (Hammer Test, UPV, etc.)
2. **Core Sampling** for laboratory testing
3. **Load Analysis** according to current function
4. **Structural Modeling** with software (ETABS, SAP2000)
5. **Capacity Evaluation** compared against requirements

## ✅ Conclusion

Structural recalculation is not optional — it is a **necessity** to ensure the safety and longevity of a building. With the right approach, you can:

- Know the building's remaining lifespan
- Plan reinforcement if needed
- Obtain building permits safely
